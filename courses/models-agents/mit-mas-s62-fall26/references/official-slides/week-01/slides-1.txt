     Reading Assignments
      • 4 readings assignments, with usually 2 required papers and some suggested
        (but optional) papers, and 5-6 discussion probes.
      • Three main assignment parts:
         •   Reading notes: Read the assigned papers and summarize the main take-away points
              •   Optional: if you have clarification questions about the papers
         •   Paper scouting: Scout for extra papers, blog posts or other resources related to these
             question probes
         •   Discussion points: Reflect on the question probes related to the reading papers, prepare
             discussion points, with live discussion and brainstorming in class.
      • Goal is to jointly write a survey paper on self-evolving AI combining each
       discussion session, focusing on a clear taxonomy and rigorous new directions.

1965 Intelligence Explosion


    Irving John Good was a British mathematician
    who worked as a cryptologist at Bletchley Park
    alongside Alan Turing.

    After the Second World War, Good continued
    working with Turing on computer design and
    Bayesian statistics at the University of
    Manchester.

    In 1965, he published “Speculations
    Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine,”
    in which he proposed the idea of an
    intelligence explosion driven by machines
    recursively improving their own design.
1965 Intelligence Explosion




        1.   A machine has the intellectual ability to design machines.
        2.   It uses that ability to design a more capable successor.
        3.   That successor is also better at machine design.
        4.   It can therefore contribute to another round of improvement.

Research Ideas and Tasks
Can LLM learn taste and make genuine innovations?

  Goal: move beyond generating plausible variations of existing ideas toward
  identifying valuable directions and producing genuinely novel discoveries.

  1.   Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study
       with 100+ NLP Researchers, Si et al., 2024.
  2.   HindSight: Evaluating Research Idea Generation via Future Impact, Bo Jiang,
       2026.
  3.   LLMs Can’t Jump, Tom Zahavy (Google DeepMind), 2026.
Can AI automate engineering optimization tasks?
Can AI automate AI research itself?
 Goal: enable agents to autonomously select ideas, run experiments, and
 produce validated research discoveries.
  1.   PaperBench: Evaluating AI’s Ability to Replicate AI Research, Giulio Starace
       et al. (OpenAI), 2025.
  2.   Learning to Discover at Test Time, Mert Yuksekgonul et al. (Stanford
       University and NVIDIA), 2026.
  3.   FrontierCS: Evolving Challenges for Evolving Intelligence, Qiuyang Mang et
       al., 2025.
  4.   CORAL: Towards Autonomous Multi-Agent Evolution for Open-Ended
       Discovery, Ao Qu, Han Zheng, Zijian Zhou et al. (MIT and NUS), 2026.
  5.   EdgeBench: Unveiling Scaling Laws of Learning from Real-World
       Environments, Deyao Zhu et al. (ByteDance Seed), 2026.
How to effectively turn experience into persistent knowledge?
 Goal: convert noisy interaction trajectories into reusable knowledge that
 persists across tasks without accumulating errors or catastrophic forgetting.

  1.   ExpeL: LLM Agents Are Experiential Learners, Andrew Zhao et al. (University
       of Maryland), 2024.
  2.   Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language
       Models, Qizheng Zhang et al. (Stanford University), 2025.
  3.   Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning, Idan Shenfeld et al. (MIT and
       Thinking Machines Lab), 2026.
  4.   Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation, Jonas Hübotter et al. (ETH
       Zurich and Stanford University), 2026.
  5.   Continual Learning Bench: Evaluating Frontier AI Systems in Real-World
       Stateful Environments, Asawa et al., 2026.
When should we update model weights, and when should
we update the harness?
 Goal: determine whether new lessons should be internalized into model
 weights or externalized via prompts, memory, skills, tools & orchestration.

  1.   GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning,
       Lakshya Agrawal et al. (UC Berkeley and Stanford University), 2025.
  2.   Meta-Harness: End-to-End Optimization of Model Harnesses, Yoonho Lee et
       al., 2026.
  3.   WHALE: A Simple Recipe for Joint Harness-Weight Optimization, Kim et al.
       (KRAFTON, KAIST & Stanford), 2026.
  4.   Mem0: Building Production-Ready AI Agents with Scalable Long-Term
       Memory, Chhikara et al. (Mem0), 2025.
How can we oversee increasingly autonomous systems?
 Goal: maintain meaningful human control when agents operate over long
 horizons, exceed their supervisors in capability, and may learn to exploit
 or circumvent their evaluators.
  1.   AI Safety via Debate, Geoffrey Irving, Paul Christiano, and Dario Amodei
       (OpenAI), 2018.
  2.   Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Eliciting Strong Capabilities with Weak
       Supervision, Collin Burns et al. (OpenAI), 2023.
  3.   Agentic Misalignment: How LLMs Could Be Insider Threats, Anthropic, 2025.
  4.   CoT Red-Handed: Stress Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitoring, Benjamin
       Arnav et al. (Google DeepMind), 2025.
Self-Evolving AI for Science and Engineering

  Goal: continually learn from simulations, experiments, and real-world
  feedback to accelerate scientific discovery and engineering optimization.

  1.   GeneBench-Pro: Evaluating Multistage Statistical Reasoning in Genomics,
       Quantitative Biology, and Translational Biomedicine, Jeremy Li and Andrew
       Ho (OpenAI), 2026.
  2.   Coscientist: Autonomous Chemical Research with Large Language Models,
       Boiko et al., 2023.
  3.   AnalogAgent: Self-Improving Analog Circuit Design Automation with LLM
       Agents, Zhixuan Bao et al., 2026.
  4.   Autonomous Agents Coordinating Distributed Discovery Through Emergent
       Artifact Exchange, Wang et al., 2026.
Self-Evolving AI for Personalization

  Goal: continually learn from individual interactions and feedback to adapt
  to each user’s evolving needs, preferences, and goals.

  1.   Learning Personalized Agents from Human Feedback, Kaiqu Liang et al.,
       2026.
  2.   OpenClaw-RL: Train Any Agent Simply by Talking, Yinjie Wang et al., 2026.
  3.   MIRIX: Multi-Agent Memory System for LLM-Based Agents, Yu Wang and Xi
       Chen, 2025.
  4.   StuLife: Building Self-Evolving Agents via Experience-Driven Lifelong
       Learning, ECNU-ICALK, 2026.
Self-Evolving AI for Multimodal & Embodied World

  Goal: continually learn from multimodal perception and physical
  interaction to adapt their knowledge, skills, and behavior in dynamic
  real-world environments.
  1.   Self-Evolving Embodied AI, Tongtong Feng, Xin Wang, and Wenwu Zhu,
       2026.
  2.   Voyager: An Open-Ended Embodied Agent with Large Language Models,
       Guanzhi Wang et al. (NVIDIA and Caltech), 2023.
  3.   Seeing, Listening, Remembering, and Reasoning: A Multimodal Agent with
       Long-Term Memory, Long et al., 2025.
